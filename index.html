PhD student, Electrical Engineering, North Carolina State University

My research interests are in physically grounded 3D and 4D reconstruction. I want to recover how deformable and articulated objects look, how they move, and what they are made of, using Gaussian splatting and differentiable physics simulation. I am also interested in world models and physical AI for robotics.

Research

Learning Physical Parameters from Visual Dynamics Ongoing

Devadharshini Ayyappan, Edgar Lobaton

ARoS Lab, NC State

Converts Gaussian and point-based reconstructions into MPM particles and meshes, then fits material parameters with differentiable simulation in NVIDIA Warp and Newton. Synthetic reference videos of object–force interactions supervise the physical consistency of the result.

Non-Rigid Multi-Part Triplane Gaussian Splatting

Research internship, Dolby Laboratories, 2026

Extends multi-part triplane Gaussian splatting from rigid articulation to non-rigid deformation. Soft boundary blending between parts and a learned residual deformation module model bending and stretching beyond rigid joint transforms. Evaluated on a new Blender dataset of deformable articulated objects, where it improves reconstruction and deformation fidelity over rigid multi-part baselines.

AI-Driven Mussel Behavior Monitoring Using an Accessible 3D Imaging System

M. Snap, A. Badalamenti, D. Ayyappan, S. Banerjee, D. M. Urieta, C. Cooper, M. Daniele, J. Reynolds, J. Levine, A. Bozkurt, E. Lobaton

ASEE Annual Conference & Exposition, 2025

Low-cost Raspberry Pi and ROS2 capture rigs (underwater, rotating-arm, and rotating-platform) with multi-view calibration and cloud sync. Related work tracks valve gaping with two IMUs and Rodrigues-rotation differential estimation, reaching about 1.9° RMSE on real data.
